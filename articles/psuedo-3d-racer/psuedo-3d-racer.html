Psuedo 3D Racer

There are plenty of resources on the web detailing ways to reproduce this psuedo-3D effect, but most of them assume a traditional software rendering approach where every pixel can be plotted individually. In this article I'd like to cover a method that is better suited to modern 3D rendering pipelines, based on manipulating triangles as opposed to plotting pixels.

A great case study to begin looking at this approach is the 3DO version of Road Rash. Unlike classic psuedo-3D racers, Road Rash actually rasterises triangles onto the screen, but the geometry still retains the classic psuedo-3D warping effect.

The core mathematical operation underlying this look is the shear transformation.

We start by defining the bearing of a given location along the track. This can come from an external source or could be evaluated procedurally using a coherent noise function.

The magnitude of the shear is the definite integral of the bearing from the current view location to the vertex.
